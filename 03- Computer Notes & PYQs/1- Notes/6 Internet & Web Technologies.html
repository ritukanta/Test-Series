Network Types

  1. Internet: Global Interconnections of computer systems (Public Network).
  2. Intranet: Mainly used within businesses and organizations (Private Network).
  3. Extranet: Allows authorized users from outside to access the intranet of an organization.

Surface, Deep & Dark Web

Surface Web

  • Everything visible on the surface.
  • Comprises just 4% of the entire internet.
  • Examples: Google, Bing, Facebook, YouTube, Wikipedia, Blogs.

Deep Web

  • Parts not fully accessible through standard search engines.
  • Includes non-indexed pages.
  • Fee-for-service (OTT platforms).
  • Private Databases (Dropbox, PayPal).

Dark Web

  • Encrypted content not indexed by conventional search engines.
  • Requires specific tools like TOR Browser.
  • Associated with Ransomware, Bitcoin Services, Financing Frauds, and Illegal activities.

Chapter 3: E-Banking

Meaning and Concept of E-Banking

  • Electronic banking (e-banking) provides banking information, facilities, and transactions through electronic channels, eliminating the need to visit physical branches for every service.
  • Channels include: Internet, mobile apps, ATMs, payment cards, telephone services, and electronic payment networks.
  • Digital Banking: A broader term overlapping with e-banking but may include wider end-to-end digital services.
  • Common Features: Remote access, electronic processing, reduced dependence on physical cash/cheques, digital receipts, and mandatory authentication (PIN, OTP, biometrics).
  • Important Distinction: E-banking is an umbrella term. Systems like NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, and UPI are underlying transfer systems accessed via e-banking channels.

Types and Channels of E-Banking

  • Internet/Online Banking: Accessed via bank's official website using a browser. Requires secure login.
  • Mobile Banking: Accessed via official mobile apps for fund transfers, bill payments, and card controls.
  • ATM (Automated Teller Machine): Self-service terminal for cash withdrawal, deposits, and inquiries using a card and PIN.
  • Debit Card: Payment card linked directly to a deposit account. Funds are deducted immediately.
  • Credit Card: Payment card spending against an issuer-provided credit line, subject to repayment and billing terms.
  • Telephone/Phone Banking: Calling a bank's official customer service for basic operations.
  • SMS Banking/Alerts: Notifications or specific requests delivered via text message. (Do not treat incoming SMS as concrete proof of payment).
  • EFT (Electronic Funds Transfer): General term for electronically moving funds between accounts.
  • POS (Point of Sale): Terminal used by merchants to accept debit/credit cards or contactless payments.
  • PPIs (Prepaid Payment Instruments): Value stored in advance (e.g., wallets, prepaid cards).
  • QR-Code Payments: Quick Response codes scanned via supported payment apps.
  • Assisted Digital Banking: Utilizes Business Correspondents (BCs) and micro-ATMs for basic transactions.

Important Electronic Fund-Transfer Systems in India

NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer)

  • Nationwide centralized system operated by RBI.
  • Transactions processed through batches (not real-time individual settlement).
  • Available 24x7 throughout the year.
  • No universal upper cap or minimum imposed by the system.

RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement)

  • Operated by RBI, primarily designed for large-value transfers.
  • Processes continuously in real time on a transaction-by-transaction (gross) basis.
  • Minimum transfer: ₹2,00,000. No RBI-set maximum ceiling.
  • Available 24x7 throughout the year.

IMPS & UPI

  • IMPS (Immediate Payment Service): Instant electronic transfer offered through NPCI. Available 24x7.
  • UPI (Unified Payments Interface): NPCI system linking multiple bank accounts to one app. Uses a Virtual Payment Address (VPA/UPI ID). Requires UPI PIN only for sending money, not receiving.

Other Systems

  • AEPS: Aadhaar Enabled Payment System for basic services using biometric authentication.
  • USSD: Unstructured Supplementary Service Data (e.g., *99#) operating without mobile internet.
  • NACH/ECS: National Automated Clearing House / Electronic Clearing Service for bulk or repetitive recurring payments.

Core Banking and Important Terms

  • CBS (Core Banking Solution): Centralized system connecting branches to support "anywhere banking".
  • IFSC (Indian Financial System Code): 11-character alphanumeric code for branch identification (4 bank chars, 1 zero, 6 branch chars).
  • MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition): 9-digit code historically used for automated cheque processing.
  • SWIFT/BIC: Global financial messaging network for international instructions. BIC identifies the business party.
  • OTP (One-Time Password): Short-lived authentication code. Never share it.
  • PIN (Personal Identification Number): Secret number for authentication.
  • CVV/CVC: Card Verification Value/Code used for card-not-present transactions.
  • VPA / UPI ID: Virtual Payment Address for UPI routing without sharing bank details.
  • UTR (Unique Transaction Reference): 22-character reference used to trace RTGS payments.
  • BBPS: Bharat Bill Payment System for interoperable bill payments.

Risks, Frauds & Safe Practices

Common Fraud Terms

  • Phishing: Fraudulent messages/websites tricking users to reveal credentials.
  • Vishing: Voice-call fraud (impersonation).
  • Smishing: Phishing carried out through SMS/text messages.
  • Skimming: Illicit capture of card data using compromised physical devices at ATMs or POS.

Security Checklist

  • Use official bank apps or manually enter genuine URLs.
  • Never disclose PIN, Password, OTP, CVV, or UPI PIN.
  • You DO NOT need to enter a UPI PIN to receive money.
  • Verify payee details before authorizing any transaction.
  • Avoid remote-access screen sharing apps on unknown requests.

Chapter 4: Cybersecurity

General Concepts & Definitions

  • VIRUS: Stands for Vital Information Resources Under Siege. It is a man-made computer program that can copy itself and infect a computer without permission or knowledge of the owner.
  • Malware: A broad term for Malicious Software.
  • Retrovirus: A virus specifically designed to delete or disable anti-virus software.
  • Macrovirus: A virus that targets and harms application software (e.g., Microsoft Word, Excel).
  • WORM: Malware that self-replicates and sends itself to other computers across a network. Does not need to attach to a host file.
  • Trojan Horse: Software that pretends to be useful or legitimate but actually contains malicious code. It does not self-replicate.
  • Anti-Virus Software Examples: Avira, Avast, Panda, McAfee, Norton, AVG, Quick Heal, Kaspersky (Russian provider headquartered in Moscow).

Historical Firsts

  • First Computer Virus: Creeper (Created by Bob Thomas, 1971).
  • First Computer Worm: The Morris Worm.
  • First Boot-Sector Virus: Brain (1986, created by Basit & Amjad Farooq Alvi, Pakistan).
  • First Ransomware: AIDS Trojan, also known as PC Cyborg (1989, by Joseph L. Popp).
  • First Microcomputer Virus: Elk Cloner.
  • ILOVEYOU Virus: Also known as Love Bug (Created by Onel De Guzman in 2000), widely spread through emails.

Cyber Attack Techniques

Phishing & Deception

  • Phishing: Attempting to get sensitive information (cards, passwords) via digital deception (impersonation).
  • Smishing: Phishing specifically executed via SMS or text messages (often imitating Banks).
  • Vishing: Phishing carried out via Voice Calls or VoIP.
  • Pharming / Spoofing: Faking identity (Email, IP, DNS) or installing malicious code to redirect users to fake websites.

System Exploits & Networks

  • Sniffing (Eavesdropping): Capturing small data packets flowing over a network.
  • Skimming: Hardware method of copying magnetic stripe card data during physical transactions.
  • Trap Door: A security hole intentionally or accidentally left in software by a designer.
  • Logic Bomb: Code that triggers a malicious action upon a specific event.
  • Time Bomb: Code that activates on a specific date or time.
  • Botnet: A "robot network" of infected automated computers. The infected systems are called Zombies.

  • Stealing ideas or creations of others is called Plagiarism.
  • Software tracking habits and sending unwanted popups is Adware.
  • Malware designed specifically to gain administrative access is known as Rootkits.
  • Secret words or numbers used for digital protection are Passwords.
  • Repeatedly harassing someone by following or writing online is Stalking.
  • WPA2 is a standard used for security in Wi-Fi networks.
  • PGP (Pretty Good Privacy) is heavily used in Email Security.
  • The Information Technology (IT) Act in India was notified in 2000 and amended in 2008.

  • A virus capable of changing its type and signature to evade detection is Polymorphic.
